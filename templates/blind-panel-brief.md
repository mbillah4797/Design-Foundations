# Template: a blind design-review brief

For getting a design judgement that is not your own ([2.6](../docs/02-research-and-design-without-bias.md#26-get-fresh-eyes-from-other-model-families-blind-on-pictures-only), [case study 3](../case-studies/03-a-design-call-made-by-looking.md)).

How to run it:

- Use reviewers from model families other than the one that wrote most of the design. Leave that family off the panel.
- Each reviewer gets a fresh session, this brief, a folder of screenshots, and nothing else.
- Give each reviewer a different lens (for example: a creative director, a working user from your domain, an art director from a neighbouring field).
- Use real screenshots: signed-out and signed-in, laptop and phone, first screen and full page.
- For an options round, build every option on the same real data, strip labels, randomise order, and include today's version among them. Keep the key out of the folder.
- Record disagreement. Do not average it.
- Distil the reports against your floors (the rules the reviewers do not know about) before anything becomes work.

---

```markdown
# A fresh-eyes design review

You are reviewing a product you have never seen. Look ONLY at the image files in <folder>.
Do not open any other file, folder, repository or document on this machine, and do not search
for anything about the product. Do not change or create any file except your report at <path>.

## The product
<One paragraph: what it is, who uses it, how they arrive, what signed-out and signed-in people see.>

Files are named <who>-<page>-<width>.png (first screen) and <who>-<page>-<width>-full.png (whole page).

## Your lens
<e.g. "You are a creative director at a top studio." / "You use products like this every working day.">

## Prove you can see the images
Before anything else, open <two named files> and say what the biggest image shows and what the
biggest words say. If you cannot view images, stop and say so.

## Write your report in this order
1. **First reactions.** For the first screen of each main page: what you remember, what kind of product you would guess it is. Gut only.
2. **Scores.** For each page type, 1 to 10 on: memorable, desirable, clear, trustworthy, alive, distinctive (swap the logo: could this be any other product?). One table. Be harsh: a 7 means genuinely good work by a top studio.
3. **Template test.** Every element that looks like a stock pattern or an AI-generated default. Name the element and the file.
4. **The signature.** What single visual idea does this product own today, if any? What could it own that nobody else in its space has, and why does it belong to this subject?
5. **Ranked moves.** The changes that would most raise the quality, ranked. For each: where, what changes, why someone would notice, effort (S, M, L). You are free to break anything about the current look.
6. **What to remove.** Anything that lowers the feeling of quality, even if useful.
7. **Outside references.** Real products, broadcasts, games, print or brands outside this field, and exactly which idea is worth learning from.
8. **The blind spot.** The most likely reason this team cannot see its own biggest design problem.

Be specific: name files and elements. No praise that does not change a decision. Do not ask questions.
End your report with a line reading exactly: DONE
```

---

## For an options round

Replace sections 3 to 7 with:

```markdown
3. **Rate each option** (labelled only by number) 1 to 10 on: want to open it, clean, premium, readable, fits the subject. One table.
4. **Rank the options.** Best to worst, one line of reason each.
5. **What breaks.** Anything in any option that looks wrong, broken or confusing.
```
